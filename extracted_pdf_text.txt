Problem Statement 1 - Personalized Daily News Digest
PS-01 - End-of-Day Summarized News Email for Busy Users
Persona: Consider a busy professional (for example, “Arjun”) who follows news
across politics, technology, business, and sports, but has almost no time during the
day to read full articles.
Scenario: Arjun starts work at 9 AM. Throughout the day, dozens of news articles
appear on his phone, but meetings, deadlines, and travel leave him no time to read.
By 8 PM he wants one short email that covers everything important from the day,
grouped by topic, easy to skim in under 5 minutes, so he can stay informed without
opening twenty tabs.
Background: Important updates arrive all day long. Reading every article is
impractical. A single, well-organized summary at the end of the day would save time
and still keep the user informed.
Problem: Build an ML-based system that collects a day’s news articles (from a
provided dataset or sample feed), groups related stories, and generates a clear end-of-
day digest that could be delivered as an email newsletter.
Expected Output:
 Topic-wise or ranked summary of the day’s news
 Short digest text suitable for an email body (subject line + sections)
 Evaluation of summarization quality (e.g., ROUGE / human readability notes) or
clustering quality
 A simple demo: input day’s articles to output digest / mock email
Suggested Techniques: Text summarization (extractive or abstractive), topic
clustering, keyword extraction.
Suggested Data: Public news datasets / RSS-style article collections / a curated
sample of same-day articles.

Problem Statement 2 - 1990s Photo Style Generator
PS-02 - Build Your Own Retro Photo Generator
Persona: Consider a student (for example, “Meera”) who loves the 1990s retro photo
trend and wants to create vintage-style portraits and social media posts.
Scenario: Meera tries to generate 1990s-style photos using ChatGPT or Gemini,
but the free tier limits how many images she can create per day. She wants her own
simple tool where she can enter a prompt and get retro photo-style images anytime,
without depending on paid chat limits.
Background: The 1990s photo look (film grain, soft flash, faded colors, old camera
feel) is popular online. Many students use AI chat tools for this, but free plans run
out quickly. A lightweight student-built generator can solve this problem.
Problem: Build an ML / generative AI tool that takes a text prompt and generates
images in a 1990s vintage photo style. The solution should be runnable by students
on a normal laptop or free cloud notebook, without relying on ChatGPT/Gemini
image limits.
Expected Output:
 Working demo: prompt in, 1990s-style image out
 Sample prompts and generated retro photos
 Short note on model used and how the vintage look was achieved
 Simple comparison with one chat-tool output (optional)
Suggested Techniques: Text-to-image diffusion models, prompt engineering for
retro style, optional post-processing (grain, color fade, VHS-like effects).
Suggested Tools / Models: Open lightweight image models suitable for students
(local or notebook-based demo).

Problem Statement 3 - Smart Product Purchase Advi-
sor
PS-03 - Survey-Based Best Product Suggestion Across Brands
Persona: Consider a shopper (for example, “Rohan”) who wants to buy a product.
Two or more options look good, but they are from different brands, and he cannot
decide which one to buy.
Scenario: Rohan pastes product links he is comparing (for example, two mobile
phones from different brands). The system checks that all links belong to the same
product category. If one link is a mobile and another is a car (or any mixed
category), the system must reject the request and ask him to compare only matching
products. When all links are valid and same-category, it auto-generates a survey for
that category, scores the products from his answers and available comments, and
suggests which one to buy with product links.
Background: Brand comparison pages and star ratings are not enough. The “best”
product depends on the user’s own requirements. First the category must match;
then an auto-generated survey can capture needs, and an ML model can recommend
what to buy.
Problem: Build an ML-based purchase advisor that:
1. Accepts two or more product links from the user
2. Detects each product’s category and allows comparison only if all links are
the same category (mobiles with mobiles, cars with cars, etc.). Mixed categories
must not proceed
3. Auto-generates a short survey based on that detected product category
4. Uses survey answers plus available comments/reviews to recommend the best
product, with reasons and links
Expected Output:
 Category check: same-category links accepted; mixed-category links blocked with
a clear message
 Auto-generated survey questions for the detected category
 Ranking / recommendation of the best product based on survey answers
 Short explanation why that product fits the user, plus product links from available
comments / listing data
Suggested Techniques: Category classification from product title/link/metadata,
survey question generation, multi-criteria ranking, review / comment sentiment
analysis.
Suggested Data: Sample product listings with category, brand, price, features,
comments/reviews, and links (public e-commerce style datasets or curated demo
data).

Problem Statement 4 - Auto Analytics Report from
Spreadsheet Data
PS-04 - Suggest Visualizations and Generate PDF Report
Persona: Consider a user (for example, “Kavya”) who has data in a CSV or Excel
file and wants charts and insights, but she is new to data visualization and does not
know which graphs to choose.
Scenario: Kavya uploads a CSV or Excel file (sales, student marks, survey results,
etc.). The system reads the columns, suggests suitable visualizations (bar, line, pie,
scatter, heatmap, and so on), builds those charts automatically, and generates a clean
PDF analytics report she can download and share.
Background: Tools like Excel and BI software need manual chart setup. Beginners
often pick the wrong chart or miss insights. An assisted pipeline can recommend
visuals from the data types and produce a ready PDF report.
Problem: Build an ML / data analysis tool that (1) accepts a CSV or Excel upload,
(2) profiles columns and suggests useful visualizations, (3) creates those charts, and
(4) exports a PDF analytics report with charts plus short written insights.
Expected Output:
 Working demo: upload CSV/Excel to chart suggestions to generated visuals
 Auto-generated PDF analytics report with charts and brief insights
 Short note on how chart types were chosen from the data
 Sample run on at least one demo dataset
Suggested Techniques: Data profiling, rule-based or ML chart recommendation,
automated plotting, PDF report generation, optional simple insight text (trends, top
categories, outliers).
Suggested Data: Public CSV/Excel sample datasets (sales, education, health, or
any clean tabular file for demo).

Problem Statement 5 - YouTube Study Companion
PS-05 - Captions to Summary, Flashcards, and Quiz
Persona: Consider a learner (for example, “Ananya”) who studies from YouTube
lectures and tutorials, but often forgets key points and wants better exam-style
preparation.
Scenario: Ananya copies and pastes a YouTube video link. The system fetches the
video captions/transcript, summarizes the content, helps her understand the main
ideas, and then builds flashcards and a quiz so she can revise and test herself.
Background: Watching a long video once is not enough for strong learning. Cap-
tions plus summarization, flashcards, and quizzes turn passive watching into active
preparation.
Problem: Build an ML / NLP tool that (1) accepts a YouTube URL (or pasted
captions for demo), (2) extracts captions/transcript, (3) generates a clear summary
of the video, and (4) creates flashcards and a quiz to improve preparation.
Expected Output:
 Working demo: YouTube link or captions in, study materials out
 Video summary (key points / sections)
 Auto-generated flashcards from the content
 Auto-generated quiz (MCQ or short answers) for practice
Suggested Techniques: Caption/transcript extraction, text summarization, ques-
tion generation, key-concept extraction for flashcards.
Suggested Data / Demo note: Public caption APIs or a sample transcript file if
live YouTube fetch is limited during the hackathon.
